# AI for All — Trainer's Plan (4 hours)
**Audience:** anyone at work. No coding. **Promise:** by the end you can explain what AI does, catch it when it's wrong, and use it for real tasks safely.

## Master analogy — the brilliant new colleague who has never worked here
Knows everything public, nothing internal, never says "I don't know". Everything maps to it.

## Modules
| # | Module | Min | Key moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The 10-minute win: everyone uses an AI tool on a real task (rewrite an email, summarise a doc) | 30 | They see it work before theory |
| 2 | How it actually works: autocomplete on steroids; no database inside; why it makes things up | 40 | Live demo: ask about your company → confident wrong answer |
| 3 | What it's good at / bad at (drafting, summarising, translating ✓ · counting, facts, recent events ✗) | 30 | The "six things it can't do" list |
| — | Break | 15 | |
| 4 | Prompting that works: give context, give the format, give an example; what doesn't work (please/tips/threats) | 40 | Before/after prompt on the same task |
| 5 | Safety: what you may and may not paste in (data classification), disclosure, checking outputs | 30 | The salary-leak story |
| 6 | Lab: each person automates one weekly task with AI and shows it | 45 | Show-and-tell |
| 7 | Close: the 5 rules card, what to do Monday | 10 | |

## Handouts
1. One-page glossary (LLM, prompt, hallucination, token, RAG, agent).
2. The 5 rules: verify facts · never paste confidential data · state the format you want · give context · you own the output.
3. "Is this task AI-suitable?" checklist.

## Assessment
Each participant submits one prompt + output + their verification notes. Pass = verified at least one error or correctly confirmed correctness with a source.
